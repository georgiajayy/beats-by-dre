# Project 02 — Audience & Consumer Research

## Objective

Test how audiences respond to different advertising approaches, with particular attention to attention, believability and purchase consideration.

## Method

Four advertisements were tested:

1. Beats Celebrity
2. Beats Athlete / Sport
3. Samsung Everyday People
4. Skullcandy Music / Lifestyle

There were **24 submissions**. The analysis retained **21 valid responses** after excluding entries that were under-18, incomplete or showed uniform/invariant responses.

## Key findings

### Celebrity-led Beats creative attracted attention

- 8 of 21 respondents selected the Beats Celebrity advertisement as the one that attracted the most attention.
- 3 of 21 considered it the most believable.
- Its average believability rating was **3.0**, the lowest of the four advertisements tested.

This suggests that attention and credibility did not move together in this sample. Celebrity recognition may attract notice without necessarily making the product message feel more believable.

### Athlete / Sport creative performed more strongly on credibility and purchase interest

The Beats Athlete / Sport advertisement was identified as the most believable and the most likely to encourage purchase among the four tested executions.

This suggests an opportunity to show the product within a meaningful experience, where its role is clear and credible.

## Strategic implications

- Preserve the cultural recognition associated with Beats, but connect talent to a credible story and a meaningful product role.
- Explore athlete- and experience-led creative that demonstrates how the product fits into preparation, focus, training or performance.
- Assess creative across multiple outcomes. Views or attention alone are not sufficient measures of persuasive effectiveness.

## Limitations

The findings are directional and based on 21 valid responses. The sample should not be treated as representative of the wider headphone market or all Beats consumers. The findings identify hypotheses and opportunities for further testing; they do not prove that a creative format will consistently outperform another or generate sales.
